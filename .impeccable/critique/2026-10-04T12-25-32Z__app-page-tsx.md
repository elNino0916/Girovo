## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: the details are written for this product; the layout is borrowed.** The Atruvia shell is the owner's choice and fits. Inside it, the specificity comes from a FinTS client that refuses to guess:
- <Money> uses a real U+2212 minus, tabular figures and half-size cents.
- IBAN tails are set heavier, and the BLZ prints the way a bank statement prints it.
- The login field label changes per bank ("VR-NetKey oder Alias").
- A past range shows "Saldo am 30.09.2026" instead of "Kontostand".
- The Kontoverlauf is drawn only when it matches the bank's balances.
- The review shows the SEPA-rewritten text the bank will actually receive.
- "Status unklar" exists as an honest outcome of its own.

The generic parts:
- The Übersicht is the stock bank/PFM dashboard, with the balance shown twice.
- The Umsätze list is the neobank pattern (avatar, two lines, a daily net). A German Umsatzliste would show the balance after each day.
- Analyse is a stock four-KPI strip.
- The success screen is a stock green tick.

The biggest missed opportunity: the "only your bank sees it" promise is visible only on the login screen, and only at 1024px and wider. It never appears on Analyse or Verträge, where the "more than the bank" work happens, and never at logout, the one moment the app could prove it.

## Overall Impression

An unusually exact banking client inside a deliberately familiar shell. On the normal path it earns trust down to the single character, and the approval wait is among the best-designed moments in German banking software. It falters exactly where the stakes are highest:
- At decisions: the Namensabgleich mismatch and the duplicate-payment check.
- At failures: refusals, bank outages and partial loads.
- On two promises: "only your bank sees it" and "Beträge ausblenden" don't hold by default.

Single biggest opportunity: make decisions and failures as exact as the normal path. That means one set of outcome words (bestätigt, abgelehnt, unklar, unvollständig) that every dialog, tile and total uses, with the safe choice always as the primary button.

## What's Working

1. Exactness is the trust mechanism, and it holds down to the character. The user's real question is "is this what my bank reported?", and the app answers it before it's asked:
   - <Money>: tabular figures, U+2212 minus, half-size cents (Money.tsx:20-22, 99-133).
   - "Saldo am …" instead of "Kontostand" for a past range.
   - A Kontoverlauf drawn only when it reconciles with the bank's balances.
   - "Basis: GiroKomfort · 4 Umsätze · ohne 1 Umbuchung" under figures.
   - A review step that shows "Umlaute ausgeschrieben (ä → ae)".

   Generic finance dashboards smooth over exactly these gaps.
2. The approval wait is designed for anxiety.
   - It names the device the bank chose.
   - It quotes the bank's request word for word.
   - It counts elapsed time against the bank's own time limit.
   - It keeps the ticking counter out of the screen-reader live region (TanWaitOverlay.tsx:53-56, 102-112, 188-199).
   - Cancelling a transfer approval asks first, with "Weiter warten" focused.
   - A lost answer is never called a failure (FintsProvider.tsx:1823-1834).
3. Shortcuts that only prefill, on top of near-perfect field safety.
   - Strg+K, a pasted GiroCode and recent payees are fast, but every route still passes "Prüfen" and "Freigabe".
   - The IBAN field groups digits as you type, checks the checksum only at full length, and names the bank ("ING-DiBa · INGDDEFFXXX").
   - The amount field refuses "12,999" instead of guessing.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] 1. On a Namensabgleich mismatch, the risky choice is the primary button**
- What:
  - On a NO_MATCH result, the only filled button is "Trotzdem überweisen", in the same spot where "Jetzt überweisen" sat a moment earlier (TransferSheet.tsx:525-537). It confirms the parked order at once, and if the bank waives the second factor, one click sends the money.
  - The "kein Abgleich möglich", "noch in Prüfung" and "unbekannt" results reuse the copy "trotz Abweichung", although the bank reported no deviation (VopResult.tsx:64).
  - The result never shows the IBAN that was checked.
- Why it matters: this is the one regulated decision in the app, and the bank's own text on that screen says the customer carries the risk. It's the exact moment fraud victims are coached to click through. Copy that cries "Abweichung" when there's none trains people to click through the real one.
- Fix:
  - On NO_MATCH: make "Angaben prüfen" the primary button; it returns to the form with the values kept. Don't force focus to Name: in invoice fraud, the IBAN is what's wrong. Make "Trotzdem überweisen" an outline secondary. Add an IBAN row in Plex Mono, plus one line: check name and IBAN with the recipient through a channel you already know.
  - On CLOSE_MATCH with a suggested name: the primary becomes "Namen übernehmen".
  - When no check was possible: use the copy "Der Name konnte nicht geprüft werden …" with the button "Ohne Abgleich überweisen".
  - Keep the bank's text word for word, and keep the result visible during the approval that follows.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] 2. The duplicate-payment warning can be out of view, and it doesn't see earlier sessions**
- What:
  - "Schon einmal überwiesen?" appears after the amount card and five summary rows (Review.tsx:161-165), below a pinned "Jetzt überweisen".
  - On the paths most likely to produce duplicates (template, "Erneut überweisen", prefilled forms), the review step opens at the top. At 900×600, and in windows about 670–720px tall, the warning then starts below the fold.
  - It is never announced to screen readers; the live text says only "Schritt 2 von 4".
  - The check reads only this session's log and booked statements: no Vorgemerkte. The log is cleared at every logout, including the 10-minute auto-logout. So someone who pays on Friday evening, sees nothing booked on Saturday and pays again gets no warning.
- Why it matters: paying twice is the most likely money mistake, and this is the only guard against it. The name check doesn't look at history, and neither does the TAN app.
- Fix:
  - Put the warning at the top of the review step and reset scroll to 0 on every step change.
  - Add the warning to the step's live text and link it from the primary button (aria-describedby).
  - Relabel the primary "Trotzdem überweisen" when the warning shows.
  - Keep a 14-day record (account, IBAN, amount, time, outcome) of executed and unclear orders in the vault encrypted with the PIN, cleared by "Gerät vergessen".
  - Also check loaded Vorgemerkte.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] 3. Pressing "Ablehnen" in the TAN app ends in "Status unklar … kann trotzdem ausgeführt werden"**
- What:
  - When the bank answers a status check with a refusal (tan-poll/route.ts:70-73), the client turns every such error into "Freigabe konnte nicht geprüft werden", shown above raw "9xxx: … | 9800: …" codes.
  - For a transfer, "Schließen" then leads to "Status unklar". The session log records it as unclear and drops the bank's reason.
  - Separately, login and statement approvals share the same generic title. At banks with generic approval texts, a second approval right after login looks like the first one repeated.
- Why it matters: the user did the safe thing and is told the money may still leave. That means a call to the bank, and it contradicts the product's core promise that what you see is what the bank reported.
- Fix:
  - Have the route return an explicit refused result only when the bank's answer is a clear refusal.
  - Map only that result to "Überweisung nicht ausgeführt – deine Bank hat den Auftrag abgelehnt:", followed by the bank's sentences without codes, with "Angaben ändern" as the primary.
  - Everything else stays "unklar": network loss, HTTP 500, "Kein offener Vorgang", a dialog abort alone, or an answer that also contains an execution code. Otherwise the fix itself invites a double payment.
  - Give each kind of wait its own title ("Anmeldung freigeben", "Umsatzabruf freigeben"), and add "Die Anmeldung ist freigegeben – für die Umsätze fragt deine Bank ein zweites Mal."
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] 4. Two privacy promises don't hold by default: company names go to Brandfetch with no switch, and "Beträge ausblenden" leaks amounts**
- What (Brandfetch):
  - Release builds ship a Brandfetch client ID with logos on (lib/session.ts:83-91), so company names from bookings go to Brandfetch along with the user's IP address.
  - The disclosure is one sentence that never names Brandfetch and only appears in windows 1024px or wider (AuthShell.tsx:67, 160-197).
  - There is no switch in the app. The README's way to turn it off is editing config.json in the install folder, which PRODUCT.md says users never touch, and which an update would likely overwrite.
- What (privacy mode):
  - Rows and the booking detail still show amounts inside bank text: "Kontofuehrung 6,90" next to "••••• €" (TxRow.tsx:68, TxDetail.tsx:145).
  - The Kontoverlauf stays red below zero, with its area anchored at zero (BalanceChart.tsx:133, 328-351, 403). That shows anyone watching a screen share a Girokonto in the overdraft.
- Why it matters: this is claim #1 ("only your bank sees it") and DESIGN.md's documented masking promise. Shop names can reveal health insurers, debt collectors or gambling.
- Fix:
  - Add a "Firmenlogos (Brandfetch)" switch next to Updates in the Sitzung panel, and use it to gate the lookups (FintsProvider.tsx:805).
  - Choose the default deliberately. If the app asks, ask before the first lookup, which currently fires on the first statement load.
  - Name Brandfetch in the disclosure at every window width.
  - In privacy mode, mask amount-shaped text when rendering rows and details, not in the cached text, as a documented best effort. Draw the chart from its minimum to its maximum, without a zero line and without red. Keep the hero balance navy.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] 5. The DKB quick-pick tile selects the Kreis- und Stadtsparkasse Dinkelsbühl**
- What:
  - The DKB tile types "DKB" into a search that indexes only name, town and BIC. "DKB" occurs only inside that Sparkasse's BIC, BYLADEM1DKB (lib/banks.ts:98, 159). It is the only result, and Enter accepts it (BankPicker.tsx:147-155).
  - Keyboard and screen-reader users land on that Sparkasse's login and send their DKB credentials there. Mouse users see a Sparkasse logo and conclude DKB isn't supported.
  - A BLZ typed the way the app itself prints it ("370 400 44") finds nothing, and neither does an IBAN. Both get "Prüfe die BLZ".
- Why it matters: choosing the bank is the first task, DKB is one of the largest direct banks, and claim #2 is "any German bank".
- Fix:
  - Give the DKB preset BLZ 12030000 and the hint "DKB-App", the way the ING tile already has one.
  - Match BICs only from their start, and add aliases (DKB, HVB, apoBank, OLB).
  - Strip spaces from digit-only queries.
  - Recognise an IBAN on the client and send only the BLZ, never the IBAN, in the search URL.
  - Make the empty state specific to the query: "Eine BLZ hat 8 Ziffern – du findest sie in deiner IBAN an Stelle 5 bis 12."
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

## Questions to Consider

- At a Namensabgleich mismatch, whose side should the big blue button be on: the payment's, or the person's who may be on the phone with a scammer?
- What if every dialog, tile and total had to use the same four outcome words (bestätigt, abgelehnt, unklar, unvollständig)? How many of these P1s would simply disappear?
- "Status unklar" is where "more than the bank" could matter most. Why not search Vorgemerkt and Umsätze for the same IBAN and amount yourself and come back with "Gefunden" or "Noch nicht sichtbar – nicht erneut senden"?
- This may be the only banking app that can truthfully tell you at logout that only your bank saw anything. Why does that promise disappear after the login screen?
